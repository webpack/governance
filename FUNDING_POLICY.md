# webpack Funding & Disbursement Policy

This policy governs the collection, approval, and disbursement of all funds held by or on behalf of the webpack project, which includes, but is not limited to:

- Open Collective (funds legally held by Open Source Collective, the project's fiscal host)

## Definitions

- **TSC**: the webpack Technical Steering Committee.
- **CPC**: the Cross Project Council of the OpenJS Foundation.
- **Fiscal host**: Open Source Collective, the legal entity holding funds on the project's behalf via the Open Collective platform.
- **Steward**: one of the two persons delegated by the TSC under the Stewardship section to hold administrator access to the project's fund accounts and to approve disbursements. The **project steward** is a person associated with the webpack project; the **foundation steward** is a person associated with the Linux Foundation (including the OpenJS Foundation). Neither steward may be a recipient of project funds.
- **Administrator**: a person holding administrator access to a fund account under this policy; that is, a steward.
- **Personal interest**: a person holds a personal interest in an expense whenever the recipient being paid would benefit that person in any way, directly or indirectly. This includes, without limitation: being the recipient's employer, employee, or contractor (in either direction); being the recipient's business or romantic partner; sharing a household or immediate family with the recipient; or holding a financial stake in the recipient.
- **Independent approver**: a steward (or a substitute approver designated under the Approval of disbursements section) who is neither the recipient of an expense nor holds any personal interest in it.
- **Regular recipient**: a person who has received project funds before and is expected to receive them again.

## Governing principle

**No person may approve, schedule, or release a payment of which they are the recipient or in which they hold a personal interest.** Every disbursement requires the approval of at least two independent approvers, and the expiry of the objection window described below without an unresolved objection. The TSC retains oversight of every payment through that objection window and the votes described in this policy; the stewards administer the funds, they do not own the decision.

## Affirmations

**By submitting an expense, a recipient swears** that they have read this policy as it stands on the day they submit and agree to be bound by that version; that everything stated in the expense is true and complete, and that the hours claimed were actually worked and the costs actually incurred by them; that compensation was agreed in advance as required below, and they have linked that agreement; that the expense has not been paid or reimbursed before, in whole or in part, by the project or anyone else; that they have disclosed anything that would give a steward or TSC member a personal interest in it; that they are not subject to sanctions that would make the payment unlawful; and that they understand a false statement here is a violation of this policy. The recipient agrees to repay anything paid to them in violation of this policy.

**By approving an expense, an approver swears** that they are an independent approver to that expense, that they have reviewed it against the version of this policy that governs it, and that they know of nothing that would make the recipient's statement untrue or make themselves biased in any way.

**By accepting delegation as a steward, a steward swears** that they are not and will not become a recipient of project funds while holding the role, that they have disclosed their employer and any sponsor ties and will disclose any change, and that they will act on this policy and on the decisions of the TSC rather than their own discretion.

**By submitting, receiving, approving, or otherwise managing project funds, all those involved swear** that they are abiding by all laws applicable to them, including United States law applicable to the payment, and any laws binding on the OpenJS Foundation or the fiscal host.

## Stewardship

The TSC delegates administration of the project's fund accounts to two stewards, designated by a formal TSC vote:

- the **project steward**, a person associated with the webpack project (such as a TSC member or long-standing collaborator) who does not and will not receive project funds while holding the role; and
- the **foundation steward**, a person associated with the Linux Foundation or the OpenJS Foundation who does not and will not receive project funds while holding the role.

The two stewards may not share an employer. A steward who becomes a recipient of project funds, or acquires a personal interest that would recur across expenses, vacates the role.

Delegation is for a term of one year and is renewable by TSC vote. A steward may be replaced at any time by a TSC vote, after written notice and an opportunity to respond, or automatically when the steward fails to respond to written notice from the TSC or the fiscal host within 30 days. Every change of steward is recorded by the TSC and takes effect when the fiscal host confirms the change of administrators. While a steward seat is vacant, the TSC designates by vote a substitute approver, who must not be a recipient of project funds and must hold no personal interest in the expenses concerned, to act until the seat is filled.

Stewards administer accounts, review and approve expenses under this policy, and publish, at least quarterly, a short public summary of income, spend by category, and balance. Stewards hold no technical authority by virtue of the role, and TSC membership carries no administrator access by itself.

## Administrative access

The two stewards shall hold "**Admin**" access to each and every fund account at all times. Neither steward may be a regular recipient of project funds. Withholding or refusing administrator access from a duly designated steward is itself a violation of this policy, except where access is suspended or revoked as an enforcement measure under the Enforcement section.

All TSC members shall hold "**Core Contributors**" access to each and every fund account at all times. They must have visibility of every expense from the moment it is submitted, including its description, amount, supporting detail, and linked advance agreement, so that they may exercise the objection right below. Withholding that visibility from a TSC member is itself a violation of this policy. All income and expenses remain on the fund account's public ledger.

## Approval of disbursements

To receive a disbursement, a recipient shall submit each expense with a description, category, amount, the statements required above, along with supporting detail such as hours, tasks, or deliverables. The two stewards, as independent approvers, shall each review the expense for compliance with this policy, and allow for objections during the below mentioned period, before it is released. Where a steward is the recipient of, or holds a personal interest in, an expense, or where a steward seat is vacant, the TSC shall designate by vote a substitute independent approver for that expense.

The OpenJS Foundation, the webpack project, or the fiscal host may, at their sole discretion, and with legal counsel, require a recipient to complete, before any payment is made, any lawful verification deemed necessary. This includes, without limitation, identity verification, background checks, proof of residency or tax status, sanctions screening, and tax or banking documentation. Payment may be withheld until required verification is completed, but refusing verification is not a violation of this policy, and payees have the right to do so. The TSC, the OpenJS Foundation, and the fiscal host each retain the right to refuse any payment.

**Objection window.** No expense may be released until 72 business hours have passed from its submission being visible to all stewards and TSC members. Any steward or TSC member may object within that window. An objection must state a reason but need not cite a specific provision of this policy; silence through the window is consent. A sustained objection blocks payment until it is resolved by a TSC vote, in which any member holding a personal interest in the expense may not vote. The stewards act on the outcome of that vote.

Where public disclosure of an expense would itself create risk (such as payment for security work that cannot be described publicly until a remedy is released) the description may be redacted to the minimum necessary. Even so, as much information as possible should still be recorded publicly with the appropriate redactions; the fact that an expense relates to security work is not itself damaging information.

## Permitted purposes

Funds may be disbursed only for the following purposes. Specific exceptions may be approved by a formal TSC vote, in which any member holding a personal interest in the exception may not vote:

- **Maintenance and development**: compensation for maintenance or development work agreed in advance.
- **Community engagement and management**: compensation for the fostering and supporting of the webpack community, including its maintainers, contributors, and users.

For any payments, regardless of task, **compensation must be agreed in advance.** An agreement counts as being made in advance only if it is recorded publicly, names the work and the rate or amount, and predates the work itself. Only work the TSC has agreed beforehand to compensate is eligible. Contributing without a prior agreement to be paid (by fixing issues, opening pull requests, or otherwise) creates no entitlement to compensation. **Contributions are an act of volunteering, and payment is not a right.**

## Rates and limits

No individual may receive more than USD 5,000 in any calendar month across all categories without a specific recorded TSC exception ratified via a vote, approved with interested members recused as described above.

Where total disbursements in a calendar month exceed one-twelfth of the project's available budget for the calendar year, no further expenses may be approved and the TSC must conduct a review before the next payment cycle, so as to prevent running the budget dry. Expenses already approved before the threshold was crossed shall still be paid.

## Enforcement

**Any failure to abide by this policy is a violation of it.**

A disbursement made in violation of this policy is subject to reversal, and the TSC will require repayment. A steward responsible for a violation may have their access to Open Collective suspended or revoked by the TSC, and any person responsible for a violation may have their status with the webpack project suspended or revoked depending on the decision of OpenJS leadership (including the Technical Steering Committee, Cross Project Council, and Code of Conduct Committee).

## Amendment

Amendment of this policy, including any change to the PTS algorithm, requires a proposal and vote by the TSC, followed by ratification by the CPC.
